# Task 30 research brief — Iowa mowing frequency

Research date: 2026-09-02 (Asia/Amman). Governing editorial policy: `docs/blog-writing-guidance.md`. Scope: Task 30 only; baseline `929c2f1`. No deployment or external-account changes.

## User intent

Decide whether an established Iowa lawn needs another mowing by comparing its growth with an appropriate cutting height, then checking whether conditions justify postponement. This is not a service-frequency offer or a year-round maintenance calendar.

## Research questions

Investigated fixed versus growth-based frequency, target height versus standing height, the one-third rule, seasonal growth, Kentucky bluegrass versus broader turf assumptions, moisture and summer dormancy, rain/dew delays, heat-of-day considerations, and whether a delayed cut changes the removal limit. Also checked official source freshness and differing recommendations, current same-intent terminology, and a small representative search-result sample. Clipping handling, equipment, treatments, irrigation prescriptions, municipal law, current weather and other article topics are excluded from published advice.

### Freshness and conflict checks

The [official lawn resource directory](https://yardandgarden.extension.iastate.edu/how-to/lawn-and-turfgrass-resources-iowa), last reviewed February 2025, still links S1, S2, S5 and S6. Targeted official-site searches for 2025/2026 frequency guidance found S3 and the [July 2026 lawn-alternatives guide](https://yardandgarden.extension.iastate.edu/how-to/lawn-alternatives-turfgrass). No replacement invalidating S1's growth/removal principle was found. This is a bounded search, not a claim that every university document was audited.

- Lawn-alternatives guidance discusses longer intervals with taller grass for pollinator/low-input objectives. Those conditions differ from a universal mowing prescription. Do not claim Iowa State never discusses intervals; use S1 to answer the narrower frequency decision and omit the alternative-management program.
- S2's bluegrass summer range and S4's drought-context height range differ. Do not flatten them into one all-Iowa setting. Publish the higher-summer-height principle scoped to bluegrass, with a link to S2; use 3 inches only as S1's explicitly conditional worked example, not a recommended height for every species, season or drought condition.
- S3 describes very little dormant growth and reduced mowing need; S4 explicitly says not to mow dormant lawns. Publish the explicit pause from S4, with S3 explaining why the active-growth interval does not carry over. Do not use brown color alone to diagnose dormancy, and do not promise recovery.
- S6 gives a usual early/mid-November central-Iowa endpoint; [Late Summer and Fall Lawn Care](https://yardandgarden.extension.iastate.edu/how-to/late-summer-and-fall-lawn-care), reviewed August 2024, gives a different usual Iowa window. Both support the same cessation-of-growth criterion. Omit month/date and temperature cutoffs; publish only that shared principle.
- The [2010 mowing news article](https://yardandgarden.extension.iastate.edu/article/2010/4-14/mowing.html) was inspected but carries a dated-content caution. Its rigid-looking seasonal intervals and detailed recovery/equipment advice are not used as publication authority. Current FAQ/how-to sources suffice for the selected claims.
- The directory's Mowing Your Lawn PDF link failed through a signed-download redirect. No unseen PDF content is treated as evidence. The accessible official HTML sources independently support every retained claim.

## Differentiation brief

Commodity coverage: frequency, removal limit, height and seasonal change are expected. Actual gap: translate those facts into the next observation and distinguish growth need from a suitable mowing opportunity.

Three supported value elements:

1. Separate the finished height from the taller standing height through a single worked example and explain what to compare outside.
2. Use the previous interval as a prompt to check again, with growth deciding whether the gap changes; avoid inventing another calendar.
3. Treat moisture delay and dormancy differently: dry conditions may reopen a mowing opportunity, whereas no growth removes the reason for the cut. A delay does not relax the removal boundary.

Planned structure: direct answer; height/example section; changing-growth section with a short fall endpoint; rain/dew opportunity section; one short service transition. No table, checklist, FAQ, generic benefits, separate sales H2 or recap. The article remains useful without search-engine traffic.
