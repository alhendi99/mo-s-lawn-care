# Task 32 research brief — Des Moines Fall Leaf Cleanup Tips

Research and editorial review date: 2026-09-01 (Asia/Amman)

## User intent

The reader is deciding when accumulated leaves need attention, whether the current layer can be managed in place or should be collected, how to organize repeated leaf fall without relying on one arbitrary final-cleanup date, and which official disposal information applies to the property's actual municipality. General turf guidance answers what the lawn condition calls for. City of Des Moines and Metro Waste Authority resources answer a separate collection or drop-off question for the jurisdiction and program they govern. Mo's commercial service scope is a third, independently verified question that belongs on the canonical Fall Cleanup & Leaf Removal page and in a property estimate.

Research questions:

- What current Iowa State guidance says leaf cover needs to be managed?
- When does Iowa State support mowing leaves in place rather than collecting them?
- What observable result shows that mulched pieces are small enough?
- Does the evidence support one final cleanup date or a condition-led sequence across the fall?
- What current collection information is explicitly scoped to the City of Des Moines?
- Which City program information is annual or date-specific rather than evergreen?
- What should readers outside City of Des Moines limits verify for themselves?
- Which disposal statements would incorrectly imply a Mo's handling method or service inclusion?

## Representative SERP/content-gap analysis

A small representative current sample was reviewed for differentiation only, including local service pages and national/generic leaf-cleanup results. No competitor page is factual authority.

Commodity coverage repeatedly includes scenic autumn introductions, rake/blow/bag lists, one or two assumed cleanups, fixed late-October or November dates, gutter advice, generic “smother the lawn” claims, long whole-property checklists and early professional-service pitches. Local pages often blur Des Moines with the metro and present company bagging or hauling methods as though they were general guidance.

Missing or weak decision support in that sample:

- an observable turf-visibility threshold instead of a calendar deadline;
- a result check after mulching rather than a generic “mow the leaves” instruction;
- a sequence for repeated leaf fall that does not invent cleanup frequency;
- a clean separation between lawn-care reasoning and disposal-program rules;
- explicit City of Des Moines versus metro jurisdiction;
- visible handling of annual municipal information as dated rather than evergreen;
- a hard separation between official resident guidance and Mo's unverified operational scope.

Competitor wording, heading sequence, dates, prices, schedules, images, methods and CTAs will not be reused.

## Differentiation brief

### What generic articles already say

Rake or blow leaves, bag them, mulch them, clean gutters, finish before winter and hire help if the job is large. These lists often add a fixed date or cadence without explaining how a reader should judge the actual lawn.

### What local results repeat

Local service results emphasize leaf volume, dates, bagging/hauling workflows and sales language. They frequently use “Des Moines area” around details that may be property- or company-specific, and they do not distinguish turf evidence from municipal jurisdiction.

### What this article will add

1. A visible-grass trigger for deciding when accumulation needs management.
2. A thin/dry/result test for deciding whether mowing in place worked, without turning it into a mowing-frequency guide.
3. A pass-by-pass planning model for repeated leaf fall instead of one arbitrary final date.
4. A City-limits disposal section that labels 2026 program information honestly, points to current official resources and tells other communities to verify their own municipality.
5. A final evidence boundary showing that City or Extension guidance does not prove how Mo's performs Fall Cleanup & Leaf Removal.
