# Blog Research & Writing Guidance

This document is mandatory for every blog/article task.

Before researching, drafting, editing, validating, or publishing any article:

1. Read `AGENTS.md` completely.
2. Read the exact current task in `plan.md`.
3. Read this file completely.
4. Read the existing article publishing model and relevant canonical service content.
5. Do not begin drafting until the Research Gate and Differentiation Gate below pass.

This file complements `plan.md`.
It does not override exact route ownership, metadata, business facts, or task scope defined in `plan.md`.

---

# 1. Core Principle

The goal is NOT to produce “SEO content”.

The goal is to publish useful, trustworthy, original, people-first information that deserves to exist independently of search rankings.

AI assistance is allowed for:

- research organization
- source comparison
- outlining
- claim tracking
- drafting
- editing
- validation

AI must NOT be used to mass-produce generic, derivative, commodity articles.

An article that could be produced from a generic ChatGPT prompt without project-specific research, synthesis, and editorial judgment is not publishable.

---

# 2. Google People-First Standard

Every article must satisfy these principles:

- written primarily to help the reader
- directly satisfies a real search/user intent
- provides substantial value beyond obvious summaries
- demonstrates clear sourcing
- does not merely rewrite existing search results
- does not exist only because a keyword has search volume
- does not use artificial word-count targets
- does not use keyword-density targets
- does not pad the article to appear “comprehensive”
- does not mass-produce near-identical article structures
- does not make unsupported claims to create authority

Passing technical SEO validation does NOT mean an article is good enough to publish.

Editorial quality is a separate gate.

---

# 3. Mandatory Research Gate

Do NOT write the article body before research is complete.

For each article, create an internal research brief containing:

## User intent

What exact question, decision, or problem is the reader trying to solve?

Do not describe the keyword only.

Describe the actual decision.

Example:

Bad:
“User searches when to aerate lawn in Iowa.”

Better:
“The reader needs to decide whether aeration makes sense now, what conditions should influence timing, and when waiting may be better.”

## Primary sources

Prefer sources in this order:

1. Iowa State University Extension
2. official Iowa government resources
3. official city/municipal sources for jurisdiction-specific claims
4. other university Extension programs
5. other authoritative primary sources only when necessary

Do not use competitor SEO blogs as factual authority.

Competitor pages may be used only for SERP/content-gap analysis.

## Claim ledger

Every factual/local/time-sensitive claim must have a source mapping.

Track:

- claim ID
- proposed claim
- source
- source publisher
- source URL
- jurisdiction/scope
- date reviewed/accessed
- confidence
- notes

If a claim has no adequate evidence:

REMOVE IT.

Do not fill gaps with “common knowledge” when the claim is Iowa-specific, municipal, operational, timing-related, scientific, or business-specific.

---

# 4. Research Source Rules

Never:

- invent a source
- cite a page that does not support the claim
- rely on search-result snippets as evidence
- copy source wording
- turn correlation into certainty
- expand a narrow source statement into a stronger universal claim
- apply another state's guidance to Iowa without explaining the limitation
- turn a city rule into a Des Moines metro-wide rule
- treat an old municipal rule as current without verifying freshness

Paraphrase sources.

Preserve uncertainty and conditions.

---

# 5. Mandatory Differentiation Gate

Before drafting, explicitly answer:

## What do common articles already say?

Identify the commodity information that appears repeatedly across existing results.

## What will this article add?

The article must add at least 2–4 genuinely useful elements such as:

- a decision framework
- conditional guidance
- Iowa-specific synthesis
- clear “when this does NOT apply” guidance
- comparison of options
- signs or observations readers can actually use
- jurisdiction clarification
- tradeoffs
- sequencing guidance
- service-vs-DIY decision support
- explanation of why common rigid rules can fail

If the article cannot articulate meaningful additional value:

DO NOT DRAFT IT YET.

Improve the research first.

---

# 6. Non-Commodity Requirement

Do not produce a generic structure such as:

Introduction
Why X Matters
Benefits of X
Best Time for X
Signs You Need X
Common Mistakes
Why Hire a Professional
FAQs
Conclusion

unless the actual user intent independently requires those sections.

Each article structure must come from the reader's decision journey.

Tasks 28–33 should not mechanically share the same H2 sequence.

---

# 7. No AI-Slop Introductions

Do NOT begin articles with generic filler such as:

- “A lush green lawn is the pride of every homeowner.”
- “When it comes to maintaining a beautiful lawn...”
- “Lawn care can feel overwhelming.”
- “Whether you're a seasoned homeowner...”
- “In today's fast-paced world...”
- “If you're wondering about X, you're not alone.”

Open with useful information.

The reader should receive meaningful value within the first paragraph.

---

# 8. Direct Answer First

For informational search intent:

Answer the core question early.

Then explain:

- conditions
- evidence
- exceptions
- tradeoffs
- decision factors

Do not hide the answer behind 300 words of introduction.

---

# 9. Conditional Advice

Avoid universal rules when reality depends on:

- grass type
- soil
- weather
- moisture
- turf stress
- property condition
- season
- current local rules

Prefer:

“Timing depends on...”

over:

“Always do X in September.”

Never turn a conditional Extension recommendation into a guaranteed calendar rule.

---

# 10. Business Fact Boundary

External horticultural or government sources do NOT establish Mo's capabilities.

Never infer that Mo's:

- performs a particular technique
- uses a specific product
- follows a particular schedule
- uses specific equipment
- provides disposal
- provides treatments
- guarantees results
- works in a particular way

unless that capability is independently supported by the approved business/service records.

Article advice and company capability are separate evidence systems.

---

# 11. No Fake Experience

Never write:

- “In our experience...”
- “We often see...”
- “Our customers usually...”
- “After years of serving Iowa lawns...”
- “We've found...”
- “Our lawn care experts recommend...”

unless independently documented and approved.

Do not fabricate first-hand experience to simulate E-E-A-T.

---

# 12. Author Policy

Do not invent:

- an author
- an editor
- “Mo's Lawn Care Team”
- “Lawn Care Experts”
- qualifications
- credentials

Author must remain omitted unless a real approved author exists.

A real “reviewed by” statement may only be used when the review actually occurred.

---

# 13. Date Policy

Never use today's date automatically.

`datePublished` must represent a real publication date.

`dateModified` must only change after a meaningful visible content update.

Do not change dates merely to signal freshness to Google.

---

# 14. Article Length

There is NO target word count.

Do not target:

- 1,000 words
- 1,500 words
- 2,000 words
- competitor average length

Write until the user's question is adequately answered.

Remove sections that exist only to increase article length.

---

# 15. Keyword Usage

Do not target keyword density.

Use the assigned primary keyword naturally in appropriate places such as:

- title
- H1
- opening/context where natural

Use natural synonyms and topic language elsewhere.

Avoid awkward exact-match repetition.

Never stuff geographic modifiers.

---

# 16. Secondary Keywords

If `plan.md` states:

“Missing from brief — research before drafting”

then research them during the authorized article task.

Do NOT invent secondary keywords during foundation work.

Do not force every discovered query into visible copy.

Treat them as intent vocabulary, not mandatory phrase insertions.

---

# 17. Headings

H2/H3 headings should help readers scan decisions and concepts.

Avoid repetitive SEO headings.

Bad:

“Best Aeration Iowa”
“Aeration Iowa Benefits”
“Iowa Aeration Services”

Good headings reflect actual questions or concepts.

Do not create headings solely because a keyword tool suggested a phrase.

---

# 18. FAQ Policy

Do NOT automatically add an FAQ section.

FAQ is allowed only if multiple genuine user questions remain unanswered by the main article.

Do not add FAQs for schema/rich-result purposes.

Do not duplicate content from the article into FAQ form.

---

# 19. Sources in the Published Article

Important authoritative sources must be visible to users.

Use:

- contextual source links where useful
- a visible Sources section

Do not overwhelm normal prose with academic-style citation clutter.

Claims that are:

- highly specific
- time-sensitive
- jurisdiction-specific
- potentially disputable

should have especially clear source support.

---

# 20. Competitor Research

Competitor articles may be reviewed to understand:

- search intent
- common coverage
- missing questions
- weak assumptions
- content gaps

They are NOT authoritative factual sources.

Do not paraphrase competitors.

Do not mimic their heading structure.

Do not make a “better” article by simply making theirs longer.

---

# 21. Internal Linking

Use contextual internal links where they genuinely help.

Anchor text must be descriptive and natural.

Do not use:

- “click here”
- keyword-stuffed anchors
- repeated exact-match commercial anchors

Article → Service links should help users transition from informational intent to a commercial option.

Do not turn every paragraph into an internal-link opportunity.

---

# 22. Commercial Restraint

Articles must remain informational.

Avoid:

- CTA after every section
- “hire a professional” filler sections
- repeated sales pitches
- fake urgency
- pricing claims
- availability claims
- guarantees

A restrained CTA near an appropriate decision point/end is enough.

---

# 23. Images

No image is better than a misleading image.

Use article imagery only when provenance is verified.

Never infer from image appearance or filename:

- Iowa
- Des Moines
- season
- service performed
- customer
- before/after result
- Mo's authorship

Use honest observable alt text.

Do not add decorative images simply to satisfy an SEO checklist.

---

# 24. Structured Data

Structured data must match visible article content.

Use only truthful supported fields.

Do not fabricate:

- author
- datePublished
- dateModified
- image
- Review
- AggregateRating
- FAQ
- location
- credentials

Schema is not a place to add facts missing from the visible article.

---

# 25. Original Value Examples

Useful additional value can include:

- “Should I do this now?” decision tree
- conditional timing table
- signs that indicate waiting is better
- weather/soil caveat matrix
- what advice changes by property condition
- jurisdiction comparison
- what is known versus uncertain
- when a service page is the appropriate next step

Original value does NOT require fabricated first-hand experience.

High-quality synthesis of authoritative sources can itself be original value.

---

# 26. Writing Style

Use:

- direct sentences
- concrete language
- varied paragraph length
- useful specificity
- natural transitions
- restrained tone

Avoid AI-style filler phrases such as:

- “It's important to note that”
- “When it comes to”
- “In conclusion”
- “Ultimately”
- “It's worth noting”
- “There are several factors to consider”
- “By understanding X, homeowners can...”
- “Whether X or Y...”
- repeated “can help” constructions

Do not mechanically remove every occurrence; remove them when they add no value.

---

# 27. Editorial Compression Pass

After drafting:

Review every paragraph.

Ask:

“What information or decision support would disappear if this paragraph were deleted?”

If the answer is:

“Almost nothing”

delete or rewrite it.

Every section must earn its place.

---

# 28. Anti-Repetition Pass

Compare against:

- canonical service page
- previously published articles
- upcoming cluster intent ownership where relevant

Check for:

- repeated introductions
- repeated service descriptions
- identical H2 sequences
- duplicated seasonal explanations
- repeated CTAs
- reused conclusions
- phrase-level boilerplate

Do not create a content factory with different keywords.

---

# 29. Cannibalization Check

The article must preserve informational intent.

It must not replace or duplicate the commercial service owner's role.

Example:

Article:
“When is aeration appropriate?”

Service page:
“Does Mo's provide Aeration & Seeding and how can I request it?”

Both may link to each other.

They should not contain substantially interchangeable bodies.

---

# 30. Editorial Quality Gate

Before publishing, answer YES to all:

- Does the article answer the real user question quickly?
- Are local/Iowa claims supported?
- Are sources authoritative?
- Is important uncertainty preserved?
- Is there meaningful value beyond generic search summaries?
- Is the structure driven by intent rather than template reuse?
- Is there no fabricated experience?
- Is there no fabricated business capability?
- Is there no fake author/date/image?
- Is the article free from obvious keyword stuffing?
- Is there no artificial word-count padding?
- Are CTAs restrained?
- Are internal links useful?
- Does every major section add decision value?
- Is it meaningfully different from related service pages?
- Is it meaningfully different from the other blog articles?
- Would the article still be worth publishing if Google did not exist?

If any important answer is NO:

do NOT mark the article `published`.

---

# 32. STOP Rule

Technical success is not sufficient.

A build, validator, sitemap, metadata, or schema PASS must never override a failed Editorial Quality Gate.

If research quality, differentiation, or factual support is insufficient:

leave the article unpublished
and report the blocker honestly.
