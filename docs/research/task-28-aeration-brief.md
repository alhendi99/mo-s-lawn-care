# Task 28 research brief — When to Aerate a Lawn in Iowa

Research and editorial review date: 2026-09-01 (Asia/Amman)

## Scope and user intent

The reader is deciding whether aeration is appropriate for a particular lawn, whether the current conditions make April or September a suitable time to act, which property conditions make aeration more relevant, and when waiting is more defensible than following a calendar alone.

Research questions:

- What timing does current Iowa State guidance give for Kentucky bluegrass and other cool-season Iowa lawns?
- Why do those windows matter for recovery?
- Which soil and use conditions affect how often aeration may be appropriate?
- Which soil-moisture conditions make aeration unsuitable?
- How can a reader distinguish a possible compaction concern from proof that aeration is required?
- Which information belongs to the commercial Aeration and Seeding owner instead of this article?

Excluded from research and drafting: fertilizer programs, herbicide or pesticide guidance, disease diagnosis, irrigation prescriptions, seed blends, overseeding timing, equipment purchasing, machinery advice, and unverified Mo's methods or outcomes.

## SERP and differentiation brief

Representative current results commonly repeat a fall-first calendar answer, generic benefits, symptom lists, and pairing aeration with overseeding. Several commercial or generic results narrow the season more aggressively than the current Iowa State source, present observable symptoms as diagnoses, or turn the answer into a service pitch.

This article adds:

1. A separation between a favorable seasonal window and evidence that a particular lawn needs aeration.
2. A compact factor-to-implication framework covering growth, use/soil context, and moisture.
3. Explicit wait conditions when soil is dry, wet, or spring turf is not actively growing.
4. A non-diagnostic explanation of runoff/compaction and a clear boundary around overseeding and Mo's verified service facts.
