# Task 33 research brief — Central Iowa lawn care calendar pillar

Research and pre-draft review date: 2026-09-02 (Asia/Amman). Governing editorial policy: `docs/blog-writing-guidance.md`. Scope: Task 33 only; baseline `938966b`. No deployment or external-account changes.

## User intent

The reader wants to understand which lawn and property-care decisions typically become relevant as Central Iowa conditions change through the year, what to observe before acting, and which focused guide answers the next question. This is seasonal orientation. It is not a twelve-month chore list, a fertilizer or pesticide program, a service schedule, or a replacement for the five supporting articles.

## Pillar role

Task 33 owns the year-round sequence: winter exposure gives way to spring review, active growth changes mowing demand, hot and dry conditions can slow cool-season turf, late-summer and early-fall conditions reopen establishment decisions, and fall growth and leaf conditions have separate endpoints. The page explains those transitions and routes the reader to the right deeper decision.

It deliberately leaves detailed ownership to:

- Task 28 — aeration timing, need, soil/use factors and moisture conditions;
- Task 29 — overseeding timing, establishment runway, contact and follow-through;
- Task 30 — mowing frequency, the one-third relationship and height examples;
- Task 31 — the spring property checklist and municipality-specific disposal verification; and
- Task 32 — fall leaf thresholds, pass/result logic and municipal program details.

The pillar must remain useful if its links are temporarily ignored, because its phase map explains how one decision leads to the next. The child guides must remain necessary because the pillar does not reproduce their tests, windows, worked examples, checklists or municipal detail.

## Research questions

- Does current Iowa State guidance support a condition-led calendar rather than fixed monthly chores?
- Which observable transitions distinguish spring review, active growth, summer slowdown, fall establishment opportunity and the end of growth?
- How can the pillar mention aeration, overseeding, mowing and cleanup without repeating their child guides?
- Which timing details conflict or vary enough to omit from the pillar?
- Which same-intent phrases describe seasonal orientation without turning the page into a rigid service schedule?
- What do representative current results overemphasize, and what decision support is missing?
- Which commercial statements are independently supported by the four canonical service records?

Excluded from publication: treatment products, fertilizer/herbicide/pesticide schedules, irrigation prescriptions, equipment instructions, species or seed selection, fixed frost or temperature triggers, exact first/last mowing dates, a month-by-month matrix, current weather, municipal operations, and every unverified Mo's method, inclusion, schedule, package or result.

### Freshness and source-conflict review

- Iowa State's current [Lawn and Turfgrass Resources for Iowa](https://yardandgarden.extension.iastate.edu/how-to/lawn-and-turfgrass-resources-iowa) directory still links the seasonal and mowing resources used here. The directory is a freshness cross-check, not an additional claim source.
- S1 says many common lawn tasks apart from mowing are better in late summer or early fall, while its lawn section also allows conditional spring aeration. Task 33 therefore never says every non-mowing task belongs to one season; it routes aeration to Task 28.
- S4 gives one usual fall mowing endpoint, while S5 gives a later usual Central Iowa endpoint. Both make stopped growth the controlling condition. Task 33 removes both month ranges and the temperature threshold.
- S1 and S2 contain first-mow and height figures. Those specifics belong to Task 30 and are omitted; the pillar publishes only that actual growth controls the recurring decision.
- S3 contains detailed irrigation, fertilizer, treatment and recovery statements. They are outside pillar intent and removed rather than imported.
- S4 contains detailed aeration, seeding, treatment and leaf-management instructions. Task 33 uses none of those details; Tasks 28, 29 and 32 remain the owners.
